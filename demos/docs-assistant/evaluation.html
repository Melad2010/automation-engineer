How well does it find the right answer?

48 fixed questions, each with the passage that answers it, were run through the same search code the assistant page uses, in a real browser. The questions include plain questions, questions in other words than the documents use, questions that need two passages, and questions the documents do not cover, which must be refused. What this shows. Combining meaning and keywords was not the best in every column on this test set. The best mean reciprocal rank came from keywords only. Meaning-only search put the right passage in the top 3 most often, and keywords alone ranked it first most often. With 38 questions, one question is 2.6 points, so the three methods are close; the combined method is kept as the default because it does not depend on the user using the documents' exact words, and on the separate tuning questions it was as good as or better than either method alone. The weak spot is questions in other words than the documents (for example “money back” for “refund”): these cause most of the misses and most of the wrong refusals below. Not-covered questions that the combined method still answered: “Do you integrate with Salesforce?”, “Is the app available in Arabic?”. Each shares a word with a real topic in the documents, so the match passed the threshold; the threshold was not changed after the test run.

Follow-up questions

6 pairs such as “What does the Business plan cost?” then “Does it include single sign-on?”. The assistant recognised 6 of 6 as follow-ups. Read alone, the second question found the right passage in the top 3 in 4 of 6 cases; read together with the first question, in 6 of 6.

Method

  • Documents. 40 documents (plans and billing, policies, product guide, troubleshooting, getting started, developer notes, release notes), split into 131 passages at their section headings. Each passage keeps its document title and heading as context.
  • Keyword search. A ranked keyword index (BM25) over the passages, with simple word-ending handling so that “refund” finds “refunded”.
  • Meaning search. A meaning index learned from these documents (latent semantic analysis, 64 dimensions): words that appear in similar passages end up close together, so a question can match a passage that uses related words. It is computed when the site is built; each question is turned into the same kind of vector inside the browser. No outside service is called.
  • Combined ranking. Both scores are scaled to the best match for the question and added with equal weight.
  • Refusing. The assistant answers only when the best passage covers enough of the question. Words that appear nowhere in the documents count against the match. Below the threshold it says the documents do not cover the question.
  • Answers. Sentences are taken word for word from the best passages, each with a numbered link to its source. Nothing is added beyond what the passages say.
  • Fair testing. The settings (thresholds, weights, index size) were chosen on 16 separate tuning questions, then frozen. The 48 test questions were written before the first test run and were not changed after it. Results on the tuning questions are shown below for comparison.
  • Size. The whole demo is 486 KB, including the keyword index (125 KB) and the meaning index (258 KB). The page answers with keyword search as soon as the keyword index is loaded and adds meaning search when the second index arrives.
